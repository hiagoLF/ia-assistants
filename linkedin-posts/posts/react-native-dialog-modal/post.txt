When building a Modal in React, rendering a div over the page is only part of the job.

You also need to manage focus, prevent interaction with the content outside it, and provide ways to close it.

It's easy to build a component that looks like a modal but leaves those behaviors out.

That's where the native <dialog> element helps.

Opening it with showModal() makes the rest of the page inert, keeps keyboard focus inside the modal, and lets users close it with Esc.

The dialog also appears in the top layer, above normal page content, without competing for z-index.

In React, you can wrap <dialog> in a component and use a ref to call showModal() and close(). You still have your abstraction, with native browser behavior as its foundation.

You still need to provide an accessible name, choose the initial focus, and keep the component's state in sync when the dialog closes.

What about the Popover API?

It serves a different purpose: showing content without blocking the rest of the page, such as an actions panel or additional information. With popover="auto", the browser also handles dismissal when users click outside or press Esc.

One important detail: <dialog> only becomes modal when opened in modal mode, such as with showModal(). Using show() or just the open attribute doesn't produce that behavior.

Before implementing everything in your next component, check what the browser already provides.
