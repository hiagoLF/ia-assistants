Do you really need useEffect?

If a value can be calculated from your current props or state, it might not need state of its own.

Think of a shopping cart: you already have the items, their prices, and their quantities. You can calculate the total directly during rendering.

Storing that total in state and updating it with useEffect adds an unnecessary step: the component renders with the previous total, the Effect updates state, and another render follows.

Calculating the total in the component body keeps it consistent with the data for that render, without having to keep two pieces of information in sync.

Before adding an Effect, ask:

Can I calculate this value using data I already have?
Is the calculation pure, without changing data or interacting with external systems?
Is it fast enough to run during rendering?

If so, a constant might be all you need.

The same applies to filtered lists, counts, and conditions that determine what to display.

If the calculation is expensive, consider memoization as an optimization. Adding an Effect to store the result shouldn't be your first step.

useEffect makes sense when synchronizing with external systems, such as a connection or a browser event listener.
